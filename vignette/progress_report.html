Topic and Data

Updates to Context

Initially, our project was based on AU’s static Academic Data Reference Book (ADRB). We have incorporated Professor Ressler’s feedback that the ADRB is “not well-suited for meaningful analysis” due to its single institution scope and inconsistent table structures.

Updates to Problem

Thus, we have pivoted the context from a just AU point-of-view to a multi-institutional one. The problem is now: “How does university student success (retention) compare to peer institutions when controlled for cost, financial aid profiles, and program specialization?” We are now utilizing the U.S. Department of Education’s IPEDS database because of its comprehensive longitudinal data.

Updates to Use Case

Our primary actors of administrators and secondary actors of students remains the same. However, their use cases have expanded.

  • Institutional Peer Bench Marking: Administrators are no longer limited to internal department trends. Ideally, they will be able to identify a set of “peer schools” using Euclidean distance to see if their institution’s retention gaps among Pell Grant recipients vs non-recipients are unique or reflect broad trends.

  • Program Strength: Users can now analyze Program Strength relative to other schools by examining HHI scores.

Updates to Data Sources

IPEDS data is now the source for our app.

Variable Overview:

Currently, our variables include the following:

Institutional Identifiers/Characteristics

  • unitid: A unique identification number assigned to every postsecondary institution.

  • instnm: The official name of the institution.

  • stabbr: The state abbreviation for where the institution is located.

  • Control: Classification of the institution (Public, Private nonprofit, Private for-profit)

Retention and Student Success:

  • ret_pres: The percentage of first-time, full-time degree-seeking undergrads from the previous fall who are enrolled again in the current fall

  • pell_pct: The same statistic as above, but for Pell Grant recipients

  • net_price_avg: The average net price for students receiving regular forms of aid (loans, scholarship, etc.)

  • net_price_pell_avg: There was no variable specifically for the average net price for Pell recipients, so the average net price for students on 0-30k income was used as a proxy.

  • hhi_score: Calculated value representing program concentration of universities. Sum of the squares of the shares of each CIP code’s enrollment.

    • Number of students in a specific 2-digit CIP code / total number of students at university

      • Then squared and summed.

Update of Ethics Review

Data Source: IPEDS and Public Federal Data

The project’s pivot to IPEDS substantially changes the data sourcing ethics. IPEDS is a publicly available federal data-set published by the National Center for Education Statistics (NCES). [https://www.rti.org/impact/integrated-postsecondary-education-data-system-ipeds]

IPEDS is reputable source for describing institutional, state, and national trends in post-secondary education and for providing students and families with unbiased consumer information.

Because IPEDS is a federally mandated reporting system that institutions are legally required to contribute to, there are no restrictions on its use for research or educational purposes. This data is intentionally designed to be publicly downloaded and anlyzed. No web-scraping of live systems is involved, and no access-restricted databases are being queried.

FERPA and Aggregated Federal Data

IPEDS reports institution-level aggregated data rather than individual student records; FERPA’s core protections around individual student privacy are not directly implication in the same way as with internal university data. Statistical data complications that do not contain information that would personally identify a student are not considered education records under FERPA. [https://www.american.edu/counsel/ferpa.cfm]

IPEDS is considered to comply with this standard. Nevertheless, the team should remain mindful that some smaller, or more specialized, institutions, may have very small Pell recipient cohorts, which could make disaggregated breakdowns sensitive. The cell suppression policy ( masking data where cohort sizes fall below 10) remains the best practice work retaining from the original code of conduct.

Program Specialization & the HHI

The Herfindahl-Hirschman Index (HHI) is a measurement tool originally designed by economists to track how competitive a market is. A high HHI score means most students are graduating from just one or two subject areas, while a low score means degrees are spread more evenly across many different fields. This is a creative analytical choice, but it comes with an important responsibility to be upfront with users about what the number actually means.

To be ethically responsible, the app’s “About” tab should explain how HHI is calculated, acknowledge that it was adapted from economics rather than designed for education, and make clear that it is a descriptive tool for understanding program structure rather than a rating of institutional quality.
