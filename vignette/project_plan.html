1.1 Topic and Data

1.1.1 Problem Statement and Context

American University’s Office of Institutional Research and Assessment (OIRA) publishes the Academic Data Reference Book (ADRB) as the university’s official source for longitudinal student data, which is essential for university planning and academic assessment. While the 2025 ADRB provides a reliable snapshot oof enrollment, student profiles, retention, and graduation rates, the data is currently delivered in a static 127-page PDF format. This is a barrier to data accessibility, making it difficult for prospective and current students, faculty, and administrators to perform department-specific comparisons or visualize the data over time. This Shiny application addresses the problem by transforming the static tables in the ADRB into an interactive tool, enabling users to filter and visualize how AU’s academic data has evolved from 2019 to 2025.

1.1.2 Status and key takeaways from the Literature Review (at least 3 sources)

GraduateAU assesses a real issue when it comes to deciding what school to commit yourself to during your time at university: How can I maximize my experience and get the best degree based on my interests, career aspirations, and competition amongst other students?  GraduateAU is meant to act as a resource for prospective students and their parents when deciding the next steps in the college journey. 

Graduation and retention rates are ways of assessing the success of a university. According to the National Center for Education Statistics, graduation rate is the percentage of full-time undergraduate students who complete their program across a specific time period [@nces_retention]. However, according to an annual report done by the National Student Clearinghouse Research Center, studies show that undergraduates have been steadily completing college in six years by roughly 60%, and eight-year completions by roughly 65% [@nscrc_yearly_2026]. College is taking longer and longer to complete, and getting more and more expensive. The Education Data Initiative is a subset of the work done by a small group of researchers at EducationData.org, that aims to collect data to be used to better understand the U.S. education system by organizing their findings in an accessible, comprehensive fashion. In a recent report published in November of 2025, they’ve found that college tuition has increased by 36.8% since 2020 and is projected to increase again by 3.25% for the 2026-2027 academic year [@education_data_inflation].

Alternatively, retention rates are the percentage of first-time undergraduates who return to the same institution the following fall, meaning that students are coming back instead of transferring to another academic institution or dropping out of secondary education entirely [@nces_retention]. An overview done by Hanover Research, a data-driven consulting organization, stated how retention rates are a, “reflection of an institution’s ability to support students academically, socially, and financially [@lynn_2025]. High retention rates for an academic institution can signal to investors, and parents that the university has infrastructure to support its students. Lower retention rates can give the impression that a misalignment between university administrators and student needs, financial challenges or ineffective student engagement [@lynn_2025]. Watermark, a consulting firm focused on providing strategies for instructors and academic institutions cited social isolation, unmet expectations, poor academic fit, and financial barriers as some of their top reasons for why undergraduate students transfer before their second year [@watermark_transfer].

1.2 Use Case

1.2.1 Use Case and Actor Description

  • Primary Actor: Academic Administrators

    • Use Case: These users would utilize the Shiny app to track longitudinal enrollment trends in their specific departments compared to the university at large. For example, a department chair will be able to filter by major to see if the enrollment trend aligns with their internal staffing and resource planning.

  • Secondary Actor: Prospective and Current Students

    • Use Case: Students will use the app to explore the popularity of various majors. A student interested in a particular program can use the app to see the incoming class profile for a particular year and the retention/graduation rates for that program to make informed choices about their course of study.

  • Tertiary Actor: University Researchers (OIRA)

    • Use Case: Researches can use the interactive visualizations to identify outliers in retention or degree conferral that might require qualitative analysis in more depth, without needing to manually cross-reference a PDF.

1.2.2 Actor Questions of Interest

  • How has the total enrollment in a department changed over the last six years compared to the university-wide growth rate?

  • Is the increase in secondary majors in a program significant enough to justify additional teaching assistants?

  • Which specific student cohorts within a college have the highest retention rates?

  • How does the graduation rate of a school compare to other schools?

  • What majors within a school have seen the most growth in incoming freshman enrollment in a particular year?

  • What does the typical profile of an incoming transfer student look like for my intended major?

  • Are there specific years where a department experienced a statistically significant drop in retention?

1.2.3 Anticipated Actor Workflow

Our app is designed to support an exploratory workflow.

1.2.3.1 1. Contextualization (Overview)

  • The user would begin by reviewing an About Section to understand the scope of the data and AU’s specific data definitions.

1.2.3.2 2. Data Filtering (Sidebar)

  • The user would use the Sidebar to set their parameters: School/College, Specific Major, and Timeframe. This ensures all subsequent tabs reflect the subset of data of interest to the actor.

1.2.3.3 3. Trend Exploration (Enrollment and Degree Tabs)

  • Academic Administrators would navigate to the enrollment trends tab to analyze shifts via interactive line charts and review Year-over-year growth tables.

  • Current students would move to the degrees conferred tab to view stacked bar charts that illustrate the popularity and outcome of their chosen major compared to other programs.

1.2.3.4 4. Deeper Analysis (Student Lifecycle tab)

  • Prospective students would examine the “Incoming Profiles”(Freshman vs Transfer) and Retention Rates to gauge the peer environment and of a specific program.

1.2.3.5 5. Statistical Validation (Models tab)

  • University researchers would use the statistical models tab to run correlation analysis or perform t-tests/ANOVA to determine if differences in retention rates across colleges are statistically significant.

1.2.3.6 6. Reporting and Methodology

  • The workflow would conclude with the user reference an Ethics and Disclaimer section to ensure any insights drawn are cited correctly as a student project and not an official AU administrative report.

1.3 Ethical Review and Code of Conduct

  • Data Sourcing & Web Scraping

    • The data used to make GraduateAU is drawn exclusively from the publicly available Academic Data Reference Book (ADRB), which is published annually by AU’s Office of Institutional Research & Assessment (OIRA). This resource is intended to provide the AU community with reliable and useful information.

    • The ethical risk is relatively low because this project is extracting data from an officially published PDF, rather than scraping a live website or accessing restricted internal systems.

      • Additionally, the OIRA won’t fulfill data requests from students for non-publicly available data for class-related or personal research purposes, so our project is bound to the scope provided by the ADRB [@au_oira_request].

      • We are also aware of AU’S Responsible Use of University Technology Resources that clearly prohibits individuals from accessing or attempting to access any account, file or software that we don’t have specific authorization for [@au_it_policy].

  • FERPA & Student Privacy

    • The most significant ethical consideration for GraduateAU is compliance with the Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act (FERPA), which protects the privacy of students’ education records, and affords students rights including access to their records, and disclosure only with consent except in certain circumstances [@ferpa_privacy].

      • While the ADRB presents aggregated rather than individual student data, FERPA considerations still apply when the data is broken down into smaller groups. Our team will be cautious in making sure that our statistical data doesn’t contain information that would personally identify a student. 

  • AU Data Classification Policy

    • AU’s Data Classification Policy gives more perspective and guidance that’s relevant to this project. This policy outlines the governance for privacy, security, and integrity of university data (especially confidential data) and the responsibilities of institutional units and individuals, regardless of where the data resides [@au_data_classification].

      • The policy classifies all university data into three tiers (Confidential, Official Use Only, and Unrestricted), because the ADRB data is posted publicly by OIRA, it falls under the Unrestricted category. However, we can’t combine with outside data sources because it could increase sensitivity.

  • AU Privacy Notice & App-Level Considerations

    • A disclaimer should be included in the app’s “About” tab clarifying that GraduateAU is a student project, and not officially affiliated with American University, to prevent confusion with OIRA’s resources.

  • Research Integrity

    • AU’s Office of Research Integrity states that it, “promotes the responsible conduct of research with high standards of ethics and accountability in planning, implementation, behavior, and information dissemination.”

      • Our team will make sure to document what we’ve done with the data so that it’s reproducible and aligns with the set standard of the ORI, and that we clearly cite where the data is from on the app’s interface [@au_ori].

  • Code of Conduct Summary

    • Data Fidelity: No ADRB figures will be altered and any derived calculations will be clearly labeled as estimates.

    • Cell Suppression: Enrollment subgroups with fewer than 10 students will be masked in the app to prevent re-identification to stay within the bounds of FERPA

    • Scope Limitation: The project will only use publicly available ADRB data, and stay within the bounds of the OIRA’s student data request policy

    • Disclaimer: The app will clearly identify itself as a student project, and not an official AU resource

    • Attribution: All data will be cited to ADRM and OIRA in the app’s “About” tab

1.4 App Concept

1.4.1 General App Layout

1.4.2 Planned Options for User Data Selection and Manipulation

The application will use a sidebar for reactive data control.

  • Variable Selection: selectInput and checkboxGroupInput menus will let users filter by School/College, Major, and Student Status

  • Time frame Control: A sliderInput will let users subset the year range.

1.4.3 Planned Options for User Exploratory numerical/graphical analysis

Each tab will function as a different exploratory tab for the different ADRB sections.

  • Enrollment Trends: Users can perform graphical analysis of enrollment data.

    • Visuals: Interactive line charts

    • Numerical Summary: A data table displaying Year-over-Year percentage growth

  • Student Lifecycle: Users will be able to visualize the incoming student to retained student data.

    • Visuals: A stacked bar chart for incoming class profiles (freshmen vs. transfers) and a grouped bar chart for one-year retention rate by cohort type (Honors, First-Gen, etc.).

  • Degrees Conferred: A categorical analysis of university attendance outcomes.

    • Visuals: A stacked bar chart showing the distribution of degrees awarded across the five schools.

1.4.4 Planned Options for User Statistical Models and Tests

The statistical models tab will allow for users to perform hypothesis testing.

  • Correlation Analysis: A reactive scatterplot where users can select two variables to visualize relationships with an overlaid linear regression.

  • Comparative Testing: A t-test or ANOVA summary table to statistically compare mean retention rates across different schools/colleges to identify significant differences in student success.

1.4.5 Design/Branding

  • Aesthetics: UI will be customized to implement AU’s color palette.
